On a farm at the edge of the village, a scarecrow stood in the middle of the cornfield with a patched coat and a pumpkin for a head. The farmer's daughter, Nell, liked him and called him Stitch. One night Nell looked out of her bedroom window and saw that the scarecrow was standing at the edge of the field, closer to the house. The next night he was at the garden gate. The night after that he was in the yard, beneath her window, and his stitched smile looked wider than before. Nell locked her window and hid under her blanket. On the fourth night, she heard slow footsteps climb the stairs, and the door creaked open. A scratchy voice whispered, I only wanted someone to talk to. Nell peeked out, and Stitch was sitting on the edge of her bed with a sad smile, holding a baby crow in his hands. By morning he was back in his field, but every night after that, a small chair stood beside him.
